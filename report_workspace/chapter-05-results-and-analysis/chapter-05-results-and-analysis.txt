CHAPTER 05
RESULTS AND ANALYSIS

5.1 Introduction

This chapter presents the empirical findings of the study using the final KSE-30 focused workflow in `6_cursor_model/`, with upstream support from `5_claude_pipeline/` where needed. The chapter follows the exact sequence implied by the methodology: exploratory data analysis, fund-flow forecasting, volatility modelling, market-efficiency testing, and a closing synthesis of the main empirical findings.

The results should be interpreted with the final sample structure in mind. The daily KSE-30 master dataset contains 1,300 trading-day observations, while the aggregate monthly fund-flow modelling dataset contains only 59 monthly observations, of which 35 months were used for training and 24 months for out-of-sample testing. This limited monthly sample is important for understanding why directional and comparative evidence is often more meaningful than large positive R-squared values in the forecasting block.

5.2 Exploratory Data Analysis Results

The exploratory results confirm that the merged KSE-30 dataset is economically meaningful and that the final pipeline captures distinct patterns in fund size, return behaviour, and capital movement. The daily reconstructed KSE-30 index return series has a mean of 0.0736 percent and a standard deviation of 1.3945 percent, with a minimum daily move of -10.2414 percent on 2 March 2026 and a maximum of 9.3245 percent on 12 May 2025. The Jarque-Bera normality result reported by the pipeline is effectively zero in p-value terms, which indicates that the return distribution is non-normal and fat-tailed. This is consistent with the later use of GARCH-family volatility models.

5.2.1 AUM Trends

The AUM trend figure shows that the tracked KSE-30 related fund universe is not evenly distributed across funds. AKD is the dominant component of the aggregate sector AUM, while NBP and NTI/NIT remain materially smaller. The raw source sheets indicate approximate peak AUM levels of 2107.24 for AKD, 94.00 for NBP, and 212.86 for NTI/NIT. This means that aggregate sector-flow movements are heavily influenced by the behaviour of the largest fund.

At the sector level, the final monthly master dataset reaches an aggregate AUM of 2414.10 million PKR by 30 January 2026. The visual pattern in the AUM plot is therefore more consistent with concentration than with equal contribution across the three funds. This concentration matters later when interpreting aggregate flow shocks, because a large movement in the dominant fund can materially shape the sector-level series.

5.2.2 NAV Return Distributions

The daily NAV return distributions are wide, leptokurtic, and clearly non-normal across all three funds. In the upstream descriptive table retained from the modular workflow, AKD daily NAV returns have a standard deviation of 2.3716 percent, NBP has 8.2404 percent, and NIT has 3.4015 percent. All three series display extreme kurtosis and Jarque-Bera p-values of effectively zero, which rejects normality.

Among the three, NBP is the most volatile by a large margin. This suggests that the underlying fund universe is heterogeneous even before aggregation. However, the final dissertation narrative remains focused on the combined KSE-30 sector-flow signal rather than on separate fund-specific forecasting claims. The distributional evidence is still useful because it justifies the use of robust descriptive interpretation and volatility modelling rather than assuming smooth Gaussian behaviour.

5.2.3 Fund Flow Behavior

The aggregate fund-flow series is visibly uneven and shock-driven rather than stable around a constant mean. Out of 59 monthly observations, 37 months record positive net flow and 22 months record negative net flow. The median monthly flow is positive at 1.8038 million PKR, but the mean monthly flow is slightly negative at -2.1598 million PKR. This gap between the mean and median is caused by one very large outflow episode.

The most extreme negative month occurs on 31 May 2024, when aggregate flow reaches -846.1858 million PKR. The largest positive month occurs on 31 December 2025, when aggregate flow reaches 233.8581 million PKR. This asymmetry indicates that the series is not simply noisy around zero; rather, it contains occasional large downside shocks that dominate average behaviour. For the report, this point is important because it helps explain why RMSE remains high even when directional accuracy improves.

5.2.4 Correlation Analysis

The monthly correlation structure is weak overall, which suggests that simple contemporaneous relationships are not sufficient to explain the flow series. Using the final monthly master dataset, the correlation between aggregate flow and monthly KSE-30 return is only 0.0095, while the correlation with CPI is -0.0262, with oil return is -0.0783, and with USD/PKR return is 0.0250. The strongest visible linear relationship is a mild negative association with the interest rate series at -0.2092.

These low correlations do not imply that macroeconomic variables are irrelevant. Instead, they indicate that their usefulness is more likely to appear through lagged or system-based modelling rather than through strong same-period linear association. This observation is consistent with the methodological decision to use lagged ARIMAX and VAR structures in the forecasting stage instead of relying on static descriptive correlation alone.

5.3 Fund-Flow Forecasting Results

The forecasting task uses 35 months for training and 24 months for out-of-sample testing. This is a demanding setting because the sample is small, the flow series is shock-prone, and the predictors include both stationary and non-stationary macro series. The purpose of the forecasting block is therefore not to claim highly stable point prediction, but to test whether parsimonious time-series models can improve on the naive benchmark in economically meaningful ways.

5.3.1 Stationarity and Granger Causality Results

The simplified ADF-style stationarity results show that `total_fund_flow` is stationary with t = -7.669 and p = 0.0000. Oil return and USD/PKR return are also stationary, with t = -7.336 and p = 0.0000 for oil and t = -8.430 and p = 0.0000 for USD/PKR. By contrast, `interest_rate_end` and `cpi_yoy_end` are non-stationary in level form, with p-values of 0.2516 and 0.3329 respectively.

The lag-1 Granger causality results are weak. Interest rate to flow gives F = 2.648 with p = 0.1094, CPI to flow gives F = 0.406 with p = 0.5265, oil to flow gives F = 0.180 with p = 0.6730, and USD/PKR to flow gives F = 0.012 with p = 0.9121. None of the lag-1 tests are significant at the 5 percent level. The interest-rate result is the closest to relevance, but it remains statistically weak.

This means that the final monthly aggregate flow series does not display strong one-variable-at-a-time predictive precedence from the selected macro variables. The later gains in ARIMAX and VAR therefore come more from combined dynamic structure and lag use than from a single clearly dominant macro driver.

5.3.2 ARIMAX Results

The ARIMAX(1,0,1)-style model materially improves on the naive benchmark. In the final KSE-30 results file, the naive model records RMSE = 261.13, MAE = 118.48, R-squared = -1.0529, and directional accuracy of 26.1 percent. The ARIMAX model reduces RMSE to 190.59 and MAE to 78.09, while improving directional accuracy to 69.6 percent.

Although the out-of-sample R-squared remains slightly negative at -0.0935, this should not be read as failure in the usual classroom sense. In a small-sample, shock-heavy financial series, the more meaningful comparison is relative performance against the naive benchmark and the ability to classify the sign of movement more reliably. On those criteria, ARIMAX performs much better than the random-walk baseline.

The ARIMAX result therefore suggests that lagged flow structure plus macro controls contain useful predictive information, even if that information is not strong enough to produce large positive explanatory power in the traditional regression sense.

5.3.3 VAR Results

The VAR(1) system is the strongest of the retained aggregate-flow models in the final KSE-30 workflow. It records RMSE = 184.70, MAE = 75.49, R-squared = -0.0270, and directional accuracy of 82.6 percent. Relative to ARIMAX, VAR delivers slightly lower forecast errors and a much higher directional hit rate.

This result is economically intuitive because VAR allows the flow variable, interest rate, and CPI to evolve jointly rather than treating macroeconomic variables only as one-way external regressors. In other words, the model better reflects the possibility that flow conditions and macro conditions co-move as part of a small system.

Even here, the out-of-sample R-squared is still slightly negative, so the model should not be oversold as a high-precision predictor of monthly PKR magnitudes. However, as a directional and comparative forecasting tool, VAR is the most defensible final model within the adopted KSE-30 aggregate flow framework.

5.3.4 Comparison with Machine Learning Models

This subsection requires careful treatment in the final report. Within the adopted final aggregate-flow pipeline in `6_cursor_model/`, no machine-learning monthly flow model was retained as a canonical benchmark. The final folder compares the naive benchmark, ARIMAX, and VAR only. This was not an omission by accident; it reflects a judgement made during the project that the monthly KSE-30 flow sample is too small to justify strong machine-learning claims.

The broader repository does include machine-learning use in related tasks, especially the stock-level rebalancing models discussed in Chapter 6. However, for aggregate monthly sector-flow prediction, the project converged toward parsimonious econometric models because they are more interpretable and more defensible under the available sample size. Accordingly, the final report should not invent a superior machine-learning monthly flow result that does not exist in the retained final pipeline.

The most honest conclusion is therefore that, for the final adopted aggregate monthly KSE-30 flow problem, classical time-series models were preferred over machine-learning alternatives because of data limitations and interpretability concerns. Machine learning remains more appropriate in this project at the richer stock-level rebalancing stage rather than at the short monthly flow horizon.

5.4 Volatility Modelling Results

The volatility section moves from fund flows to the reconstructed KSE-30 index itself. This is one of the main ways in which the final workflow becomes more consistent with the dissertation title. Instead of inferring market conditions only through fund series, the final pipeline directly models daily KSE-30 return volatility.

5.4.1 GARCH(1,1) Results

The estimated GARCH(1,1) model gives omega = 0.074916, alpha = 0.1284, beta = 0.8383, persistence = 0.9667, and AIC = 4243.43. The most important result here is persistence. Since alpha plus beta is 0.9667, conditional volatility shocks are highly persistent and decay only slowly over time.

This finding is consistent with what is typically observed in emerging and frontier equity markets, where periods of turbulence can remain elevated for extended stretches rather than disappearing quickly. For the KSE-30, the GARCH result therefore suggests that volatility clustering is a real feature of the market and not just a visual impression from the return plot.

Caption idea: "KSE-30 daily returns and model-implied conditional volatility."

5.4.2 EGARCH(1,1) Results

The EGARCH(1,1) model outperforms the symmetric GARCH specification on information criteria. Its AIC is 4209.68, which is lower than the GARCH AIC of 4243.43. The estimated EGARCH parameters are omega = 0.039982, alpha = 0.250806, gamma = -0.102610, and beta = 0.954965.

The negative gamma term indicates a leverage effect, which the pipeline explicitly reports as "YES." In practical terms, this means negative shocks are associated with stronger volatility responses than equally sized positive shocks. This asymmetry is important because it implies that downside news matters more for future risk conditions than upside news of the same magnitude.

The lower AIC and the presence of leverage make EGARCH the preferred final volatility model for the KSE-30 in this study.

5.4.3 VaR Backtesting

The Value-at-Risk backtest is based on the best-performing volatility model, which is EGARCH(1,1). Using the pipeline's 5 percent VaR line, there are 58 exceedances out of 1,300 daily observations, equal to 4.46 percent of the sample. This is close to the nominal 5 percent tail level.

This does not prove that the model is perfect, but it does indicate that the conditional volatility estimates are broadly reasonable in risk-calibration terms. In the report, this subsection should therefore emphasize that the volatility model is not useful only for descriptive persistence analysis; it also produces a plausible downside risk envelope for KSE-30 returns.

5.5 Market Efficiency Results

The efficiency results are mixed rather than one-sided. This is one of the most important substantive findings of the project. Different tests do not all point in the same direction, which means the KSE-30 cannot be described too simply as either fully efficient or fully inefficient.

[Suggested Table 5.3]
Title: "KSE-30 Market Efficiency Test Results"
Use values from: `6_cursor_model/results_efficiency.csv`

5.5.1 Runs Test

The runs test gives Z = -1.9106 with p = 0.0561. At the strict 5 percent level, this result is not statistically significant, so the final results file labels the series as efficient on this test. However, the p-value is very close to the threshold, which makes the finding borderline rather than strongly supportive of randomness.

This suggests that the sign pattern of KSE-30 returns is not clearly non-random, but neither is it strongly random in a robust sense. The runs test alone would support only weak evidence in favour of efficiency.

5.5.2 Variance Ratio Test

The variance ratio results are more supportive of weak-form efficiency. The final table gives VR(2) = 1.0069 with p = 0.8752, while the VR(4) verdict is also marked as efficient. Since the short-horizon variance ratio is very close to 1 and statistically insignificant, the random-walk benchmark is not rejected by this test.

The pipeline also reports a sub-period comparison in which VR(2) equals 1.0974 in the high-rate period from April 2022 to December 2023 and 0.9803 in the lower-rate period. Even with this difference, the broader conclusion remains that the variance-ratio evidence alone does not strongly reject efficiency.

[Suggested Figure 5.10]
Existing figure path: `6_cursor_model/figures/efficiency/EF02_variance_ratio.png`
Caption idea: "Variance-ratio evidence for the KSE-30 across alternative lag horizons."

5.5.3 Ljung-Box Test

The Ljung-Box p-value is reported as 0.0000, which indicates statistically significant serial dependence. This is the clearest anti-efficiency result in the final test set. Unlike the runs and variance-ratio statistics, the autocorrelation-based diagnostic does detect structure that departs from a pure random-walk interpretation.

For the report, this matters because it prevents an overly optimistic interpretation of KSE-30 efficiency. Even if some tests appear consistent with weak-form efficiency, the Ljung-Box evidence shows that return dynamics still contain exploitable serial structure at at least some lags.

5.5.4 Hurst Exponent Results

The Hurst exponent for the full KSE-30 daily series is 0.6559, and the pipeline interprets this as persistent. Since values meaningfully above 0.5 indicate persistence rather than pure randomness, the Hurst evidence aligns with the Ljung-Box result more than with the variance-ratio result.

The pipeline's sub-period comparison also shows Hurst values above 0.64 in both the high-rate and low-rate segments, which suggests that persistence is not limited to one short episode. The Hurst result therefore reinforces the view that KSE-30 returns are not fully memoryless over the study period.

5.6 Summary of Empirical Findings

The final empirical results can be summarized in five main points. First, the KSE-30 related fund universe is concentrated, with AKD dominating aggregate AUM and with flow behaviour shaped by occasional large negative shocks. Second, aggregate monthly KSE-30 fund flow is forecastable to some extent in directional terms, with ARIMAX and especially VAR improving materially on the naive benchmark even though out-of-sample R-squared remains slightly negative. Third, the KSE-30 exhibits strong volatility persistence, and EGARCH provides the better specification because it captures asymmetric downside effects.

Fourth, the market-efficiency evidence is mixed. The runs and variance-ratio tests do not strongly reject efficiency, but the Ljung-Box and Hurst results indicate persistence and serial dependence. The most defensible interpretation is therefore that the KSE-30 is not perfectly weak-form efficient over the study period. Fifth, the combination of moderate flow predictability, persistent volatility, and mixed efficiency evidence supports the practical extension developed in the next chapter: portfolio tilt and rebalancing application.
