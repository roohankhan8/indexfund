Your data is mixed, which is expected for financial panels.
                                                                                                                       
  - Mostly non-stationary: level series (market cap levels, average price levels, NAV levels).                         
  - Mostly stationary: transformed series (log returns, flow percentages, several monthly flow series).                
  - Borderline/mixed: a few series where ADF/PP and KPSS disagree (common in short/noisy macro-financial samples).     
                                                                                                                       
  What this means:                                                                                                     
                                                                                                                       
  - Raw levels usually have trend/unit root, so modeling them directly can create spurious relationships.              
  - Returns/differences/flow rates are safer for regression and forecasting because they are closer to stable mean/    
    variance behavior.                                                                                                 
  - Your stationarity results support the modeling choice already used in your pipeline: returns and lagged transformed
    features for prediction.                                                                                           
                                                                                                                       
  Impact on your model:                                                                                                
                                                                                                                       
  1. If you use non-stationary levels directly: coefficients can look significant but be misleading; out-of-sample     
     performance becomes unstable.                                                                                     
  2. If you use stationary transforms: estimates are more reliable, diagnostics are meaningful, and forecast behavior  
     is more robust.                                                                                                   
  3. For mixed cases: use conservative handling (difference/log-difference, include lags, and keep train/test split    
     strictly time-ordered).                                                                                           
                                                                                                                       
  Practical rule for your thesis model:                                                                                
                                                                                                                       
  - Keep NAV/price/mcap in returns or growth form for predictive blocks.                                               
  - Keep flows as percent of prior AUM when possible.                                                                  
  - Use level series only when there is a strong reason and then test residual stationarity/cointegration first.       
                                                                                                                       
  Given your current results, your dataset is suitable for modeling, but only after transformation. The transformed    
  feature space is largely stationary, which is the right setup for your fund-flow and rebalancing models.
