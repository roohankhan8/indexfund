DISCUSSION

7.1 Interpretation of Fund-Flow Prediction Results

The fund-flow forecasting results show that aggregate KSE-30 sector flow is not perfectly unpredictable, but it is also not a variable that can be forecast with high precision in absolute PKR terms. This balanced interpretation is important. The naive benchmark performs poorly, especially in directional terms, while ARIMAX and VAR produce clear improvements. However, the out-of-sample R-squared values remain slightly negative even for the better models.

This combination suggests that the underlying monthly flow process contains usable dynamic information, but that the signal is weak relative to the size of shock-driven variation in the series. In practical terms, the final models are more convincing as directional tools than as exact point estimators. The strongest evidence for this comes from the directional accuracy results, where both retained dynamic models reach 75.0 percent compared with only 37.5 percent for the naive benchmark.

The weak contemporaneous correlations and mostly non-significant lag-1 Granger results also help explain the forecasting pattern. They suggest that the selected macroeconomic variables are not individually dominant one-step drivers of fund flow. Instead, predictive value emerges only after the flow process is embedded inside a small dynamic structure. In the cleaned final run, ARIMAX produces the lowest forecast error, while VAR remains useful as a system-based comparative model.

The broader implication is that KSE-30 related fund flows in Pakistan are partially structured but still heavily affected by episodic shocks, fund-specific events, and sample limitations. The models do not support a claim of smooth macro-driven predictability. They support a narrower and more defensible claim: disciplined time-series models can extract useful directional information from a noisy aggregate flow series.

7.2 Discussion of Market Efficiency Findings

The market-efficiency evidence is mixed, and that mixed result is itself meaningful. If all tests had pointed in the same direction, the conclusion would have been straightforward. Instead, the runs and variance-ratio tests do not strongly reject weak-form efficiency, while the Ljung-Box and Hurst results indicate persistence and serial dependence. This means the KSE-30 does not behave like a perfectly random walk across all diagnostics.

One interpretation is that the KSE-30 contains pockets of weak-form efficiency at some horizons but still exhibits measurable dependence structures over others. That pattern is plausible in an emerging market context. Large and visible benchmark stocks may absorb information relatively quickly, while frictions such as lower liquidity, concentrated participation, macro instability, and slower institutional adjustment can still leave traces of persistence in the return process.

The Hurst exponent above 0.65 reinforces this view. It suggests that the market is not purely memoryless, even if the variance-ratio evidence alone appears closer to a random-walk interpretation. The Ljung-Box result strengthens the case further by showing that serial dependence remains statistically detectable. Therefore, the most defensible academic position is not that the KSE-30 is inefficient in every sense, but that it is not fully weak-form efficient over the observed sample.

This interpretation aligns well with the project's broader results. If the market were perfectly efficient in a strict sense, we would expect weaker evidence for structured volatility persistence and less room for rebalancing-oriented predictive ranking. Instead, the project finds modest but consistent evidence that the KSE-30 retains exploitable structure.

7.3 Discussion of Volatility Persistence

The volatility results are among the strongest and clearest findings in the study. The GARCH persistence estimate of 0.9667 indicates that volatility shocks decay slowly, while the lower AIC of EGARCH and its negative leverage term show that asymmetric downside effects matter. Together, these results imply that KSE-30 volatility is clustered, persistent, and sensitive to negative news.

From a market interpretation perspective, this means the KSE-30 does not simply experience isolated spikes that vanish immediately. Rather, shocks tend to carry forward into subsequent periods, creating extended high-risk conditions. This is consistent with the behaviour of many emerging markets, where macroeconomic uncertainty, exchange-rate pressure, policy shocks, and concentrated investor responses can sustain elevated volatility.

The VaR backtest also supports the usefulness of the volatility models beyond mere description. With 58 exceedances out of 1,300 daily observations, the preferred EGARCH specification produces a tail-loss frequency that is close to the intended 5 percent benchmark. This makes the volatility block practically relevant for risk monitoring as well as academically relevant for understanding KSE-30 market behaviour.

This persistence result also strengthens the broader dissertation argument. If volatility is highly persistent and asymmetric, then investors and fund managers cannot assume that market risk conditions normalize immediately after a shock. That supports the need for predictive monitoring and justifies extending the project into a portfolio-tilt framework rather than ending at descriptive analysis.

7.4 Econometric Models versus Machine Learning Models

One of the most important methodological lessons from the project is that model choice must match data structure. For aggregate monthly KSE-30 sector flow, the final project converged toward econometric models such as ARIMAX and VAR rather than machine-learning models. This was not because machine learning is unimportant in finance, but because the effective monthly sample is too small to support confident generalization from more flexible models.

In this context, econometric models offer three advantages. First, they are interpretable, which matters in an academic dissertation. Second, they are more stable under small-sample conditions. Third, they fit naturally with the economic logic of lagged flows, macroeconomic co-movement, and system dynamics. The final results support this choice because ARIMAX and VAR clearly improve on the naive baseline without requiring excessive model complexity, with ARIMAX now giving the best forecast-error performance after the cleaned rerun.

However, the project also shows that machine learning has a legitimate role when the data become richer. In the rebalancing stage, the stock-level panel is larger and more feature-rich than the monthly flow sample. There, machine-learning models such as Random Forest can be evaluated meaningfully, even though the final results still show that Logistic Regression and Ridge remain highly competitive. This is an important nuance: the project does not reject machine learning in general. It shows that its usefulness depends on task granularity and sample richness.

Therefore, the central discussion point is not "econometrics good, machine learning bad." The stronger conclusion is that the final KSE-30 project favors parsimonious, interpretable models for low-frequency aggregate prediction and allows machine-learning methods to play a supporting role where richer cross-sectional structure exists.

7.5 Implications for Investors, Fund Managers, and Policymakers

For investors, the findings suggest that KSE-30 related market conditions contain useful but imperfect signals. The aggregate flow models should not be treated as precise return-generation engines, yet they do provide directional information that may help with timing, risk awareness, and gradual portfolio tilt. The rebalancing block is especially relevant here because it translates statistical evidence into stock-level ranking signals.

For fund managers, the project offers two practical insights. First, volatility persistence means that risk management should remain active after shocks rather than assuming automatic mean reversion. Second, index composition changes appear sufficiently structured for pre-rebalancing monitoring to be useful. Managers tracking or benchmarking against the KSE-30 may benefit from watching predicted retention probabilities and expected weight shifts in advance of official reviews.

For policymakers and regulators, the study suggests that Pakistan's benchmark equity market is not perfectly frictionless. The mixed efficiency evidence and persistent volatility imply that information absorption and market adjustment may still be incomplete. This does not necessarily indicate a dysfunctional market, but it does support continued efforts toward deeper liquidity, stronger transparency, broader institutional participation, and more stable macro-financial communication.

At a broader level, the study also contributes to local capital-market development by showing that Pakistan-specific benchmark data can be analyzed through reproducible, script-based quantitative workflows. That is valuable not only for academic work but also for improving the analytical culture around fund products and benchmark monitoring in the Pakistani market.

7.6 Limitations of the Study

The most important limitation is sample size at the monthly aggregate-flow level. Although the daily market panel is reasonably sized, the final monthly modelling table contains only 59 observations, with 35 months available for training and 24 for testing. This limits statistical power, weakens the reliability of highly flexible models, and makes all inference more sensitive to unusual months.

A second limitation is aggregation. The final flow target combines AKD, NBP, and NTI/NIT into one KSE-30 sector-flow series. This is useful for building a coherent KSE-30 narrative, but it also masks fund-level heterogeneity. Large movements in the dominant fund can materially shape the aggregate outcome, which means the target is not a balanced reflection of all tracked funds.

A third limitation is that the rebalancing framework is predictive rather than rule-exact. It estimates future inclusion and weight changes from observed stock characteristics, but it does not claim to reproduce the full official KSE-30 methodology in every procedural detail. It should therefore be interpreted as a practical approximation of index rebalancing behaviour, not as a formal rule engine.

A fourth limitation is the absence of a full realized portfolio backtest with transaction costs, slippage, and turnover penalties. The portfolio-tilt application is useful as a decision-support framework, but it is not yet a fully validated live investment strategy. Finally, the macroeconomic feature set is intentionally compact. Important drivers such as political events, foreign participation, regulatory announcements, or sector-level shocks are not explicitly modelled.

These limitations do not undermine the project. Rather, they define the scope within which the findings should be interpreted: as a careful, KSE-30 focused applied research study with meaningful but bounded predictive claims.

7.7 Chapter Summary

This chapter interpreted the empirical results presented earlier and connected them to the broader research question. The discussion showed that aggregate KSE-30 fund flows are partially predictable in directional terms, that the KSE-30 displays mixed evidence on weak-form efficiency, and that volatility is highly persistent with meaningful downside asymmetry. It also explained why the final project favors econometric models for aggregate flow prediction while using machine learning more selectively in the richer rebalancing setting.

Overall, the discussion supports a balanced final interpretation. The Pakistani KSE-30 environment is neither fully random nor fully predictable. Instead, it contains pockets of exploitable structure that are strong enough to support disciplined forecasting, risk modelling, and rebalancing analysis, but not so strong that the study can claim universal or high-precision predictability.
